## What the review is for

A Canvas course export holds everything a reviewer needs to judge a course's assessments, but it is spread across
hundreds of files and written in internal identifiers. The review reads it into one set of facts, compares the layers
that have to agree, and writes them out as a plain-text file.

The review **reports facts and differences**. It does not decide that something is wrong. Where two layers disagree,
the file says they disagree and shows both values with the file each came from.

## The output file

The review file is the primary output. It is one plain-text Markdown file, and it is written so that someone who
cannot open the `.imscc` — a colleague, or an LLM assistant — can work from it alone.

The section order is fixed:

| #   | Section                       | What is in it                                                                                 |
| --- | ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Export identity               | Course title, code, term, export date, source file, due-date range                            |
| 2   | About this file               | What is included, what is deliberately not, and how to read the markers                       |
| 3   | What was read                 | File counts, modules, assessments, quizzes, rubrics, and the files that failed                |
| 4   | Gradebook structure           | Weighting, groups, weights, item counts, point totals                                         |
| 5   | Module map                    | Every module, in course order, with its items                                                 |
| 6   | Assessment summary            | One row per graded item, with its module, group, points, rubric, and state                    |
| 7   | Quiz table                    | One row per quiz, with its engine, where its questions come from, and its points              |
| 8   | Quiz consistency              | The comparison groups, their settings, and the values that differ                             |
| 9   | Facts by review step          | The facts grouped by the step they belong to: points, naming, quiz settings, rubrics, usage   |
| 10  | Assessment details            | One block per graded item: settings, the full instructions, the full rubric, and its findings |
| 11  | Rubric inventory              | Every rubric, what uses it, and which are copies of each other                                |
| 12  | Legacy and unused content     | What looks like a leftover, with the reasons                                                  |
| 13  | Pages and discussions         | Their names, modules, and states                                                              |
| 14  | Not readable from this export | Every gap, stated plainly                                                                     |
| 15  | `[REVIEW REQUIRED]` summary   | Everything the reviewer has to resolve, in one place                                          |

The instructions in section 10 are written out in full. They are never trimmed, because a requirement that was cut
off is a requirement nobody can check.

## The checks

Every check names the two values it compared, says where each came from, and says whether they match.

### A — The scoring layers

Canvas scores a course in three layers: the points on each question or criterion, the points on the assignment, and
the way groups roll up into a grade. Errors live where the layers disagree.

| Check | Compares                                                               |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A1    | A rubric's criteria against the rubric's own stated total              |
| A2    | A rubric against the assignment it is attached to                      |
| A3    | A quiz's questions against the quiz's points possible                  |
| A4    | A point value in the written instructions against the item's points    |
| A5    | The highest rating level in a criterion against the criterion's points |
| A6    | The points in each assignment group                                    |
| A7    | The group weights against 100%                                         |
| A8    | Each group's share of the grade against its share of the points        |
| A9    | Items with no points, no group, or no gradebook data                   |

A quiz's points are worked out from its **selections**, not from the point values on its questions. Canvas records
both, and they can disagree — a question can carry a placeholder value that the selection then overrides. Where the
export does not say how many questions a selection takes, the total cannot be worked out, and the review says so
rather than guessing.

A rubric that is attached but not used for grading does not set the score, so its total is not expected to match the
assignment's points. A rubric whose levels are a range is scored out of the assignment's points rather than its own.

### B — What is in use and what is left over

Courses accumulate: old rubrics, copied assignments, unused groups, unpublished drafts. The review sorts everything
into three states — **in use**, **probably legacy**, and **unclear** — and gives the reasons for every one.

The signals it weighs are: whether a module points at it, whether that module is published, whether it has a due
date, whether it sits in a weighted group, whether a rubric is attached to it, whether its title follows the course's
own naming pattern, whether it is a copy of something else, and whether anything else links to it.

Nothing is hidden or deleted. Legacy items stay visible, separated, and can be overruled.

### C — The quizzes

The review classifies every quiz by engine (Classic, New, Survey, or unknown), by where its questions come from
(stored in the quiz, drawn from item banks, or both), by its question-type mix, and by how completely the export
describes it.

Quizzes of the same kind are grouped together — by title pattern first, and by engine, points, and assignment group
when the title stands alone. Each group gets a matrix with one row per quiz and one column per setting, and the
values that differ from the group's most common one are listed as plain statements.

The review also compares the written instructions with the settings. It looks for statements about attempts, the
time limit, shuffling, and points, shows the sentence each statement came from, and compares it with the setting.
When the instructions say nothing about a setting, the institution's default rule applies — by default, answers
should be shuffled, and the time limit and the number of attempts are not reviewed — and the review says that it
applied the rule.

### D — Rubrics and instructions

The review breaks the instructions into discrete requirements, matches them against the rubric's criteria and levels
in both directions, and reports the requirements nothing covers and the criteria nothing asked for. It compares the
numbers in the two documents — page counts, word counts, source counts, and so on — and reports the ones that
disagree.

It also reports the structural facts: criteria with no name, levels with no description, levels whose points do not
run in descending order, criteria with only one level, criteria with different numbers of levels, and the same
level text used twice in one criterion.

The institution's own list of retired terms and its list of words to look at are both applied when they are set.

### E — Naming

The review compares the module item's title with the title the item carries itself, the assessment's title with its
rubric's title, the course's title pattern against the titles that do not follow it, a group's name with the names
of what is in it, a week number in a title with the module it sits in, and the spacing and punctuation of every
title.

## What is deliberately left out

- **Quiz question text, answer choices, and answer keys.** Judging those needs a subject matter expert.
- **Learner data.** The export does not carry names, submissions, or grades.
- **The live state of the course.** Overrides, section dates, applied late policies, and mastery paths are not in
  the export, so the review describes the export and not the course as it stands.
- **Instructional design.** The order of the modules, the fit between objectives and activities, and accessibility
  are outside this review.

Each of these is listed in section 14 of the file, so nothing looks complete when it is not.

## The rules the institution can set

The review takes three configurable rules, which apply to every course it is pointed at:

- Whether answers should be shuffled when the instructions are silent.
- Whether the time limit is reviewed when the instructions are silent.
- Whether the number of attempts is reviewed when the instructions are silent.

It also takes two lists: the terms the institution has retired, and the words that ask the reviewer to look harder.
